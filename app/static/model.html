Model & Evidence

How engagement is estimated, how well it works on a public benchmark, and where it falls short. All numbers were measured on the official DAiSEE test split in a single run.

Pipeline

5 frames per second · 10 s window
1
CaptureFrames from the camera, brightened with CLAHE when the scene is dark.
2
Face landmarksMediaPipe Face Landmarker: 478 points, 52 blendshapes, head pose matrix.
3
SignalsHead yaw, pitch, roll; eye openness; gaze; blinks; brow and mouth; face motion.
4
Window modelStatistics over 10 s feed a logistic-regression classifier: engaged or not.
5
AggregateScores are combined into one group figure, and only if at least k people are present.

What drives predictions

SHAP · XGBoost

Mean absolute SHAP value of the 15 most influential features, four-level task. Eye-related features lead: variation in the left blink score, the share of closed-eye frames (PERCLOS) and variation in the right-eye inward-look score. These show what the model uses, not that they cause engagement.

Honest limitations

  • Modest accuracy. Four-level macro-F1 is 0.33 at best. DAiSEE labels are noisy and the classes are very unbalanced.
  • Rare classes. Level 0 is 0.2% of the test set (about 4 clips), so its recall is nearly meaningless.
  • Single run. One seed, default settings, no significance test. Small gaps between models should not be read as wins.
  • Domain gap. Trained on one person facing a laptop. A classroom camera showing many people at an angle has not been validated yet.
  • Group level only. The product reports audience trends, not judgements about any person.
